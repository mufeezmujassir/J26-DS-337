# Research Novelty and Contributions

Defensible implemented contributions are the combination of a Sri Lanka-specific structured/vector tourism knowledge base; semantic cold-start retrieval; four traceable local context dimensions; explicit missing-evidence abstention; climate redundancy control in R3; rank-movement explanations; and a resumable, tool-grounded LangGraph research workflow.

Standard techniques include Sentence Transformer embeddings, cosine retrieval, weighted averaging, Prophet, rules, FastAPI and state graphs. The research contribution is primarily their domain-specific evidence-aware integration and evaluation design, not invention of these techniques.

Experimentally demonstrated: code/test invariants, running services, real database/vector counts, small shadow pilots, and exploratory seasonality ranking artifacts. Not demonstrated: independently validated end-user relevance, production uplift, generalization, group-emotion benefit, or world-first novelty.

Safe PP1 claim: “We implemented and tested an experimental Sri Lanka-aware semantic recommendation framework with weather, time, holiday/event and historical-seasonality evidence, plus LangGraph coordination and explicit provenance.” Avoid: “The system is more accurate,” “all five contexts are complete,” or “AI predicts the best attraction” until prospective evaluation.

Component 1 owns retrieval/context/orchestration. Group emotion remains a separate incomplete contract and should not be presented as integrated Component 1 output. Dynamic planning/transport code elsewhere in the repository is not automatically a Component 1 contribution.
